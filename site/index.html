What the percentage means

The chance that a player finishes inside the top group at his position (), by average points over his team's next four games. It is not a start/sit call: pick the level of risk you are comfortable with.

FLEX pools running backs, receivers and tight ends into one list, ranked together, so you can compare across positions.

The bar colour shows how strong the chance is: 75% and up is strong, 50-74 solid, 25-49 risky, under 25 a long shot. The tick marks on each bar sit at 25, 50 and 75. Colours are absolute, so kickers and defenses, which are hard to predict, mostly show as risky. In past seasons players in each band finished in the top group about 12% (under 25), 37% (25-49), 62% (50-74) and 78% (75+) of the time.

Predicted points is the model's estimate of his average half-PPR points per game over those four games, counting missed games as zero. It runs lower than a one-game projection by design.

What goes in

Recent points, carries, targets and pass attempts; betting lines for the next game (total, implied team points, home or away); on the Friday run, the final injury report. Nothing else: no scheme, depth-chart or news information.

How much to trust it

On 2021-2025 history the model beat simple rules by a small margin (roughly 0.01-0.02 in AUC). Stated probabilities matched real outcomes within about 3-5 points on average. The edge is modest: use it as one input, and trust the Track record tab over the backtest once it has data.

What it is best at: finding and keeping value over the next month (waiver claims, hold or drop). In past seasons its top waiver picks outscored the picks a plain season average would make by about 1.4 points per game at QB and 1.6 at RB (reliable across all five seasons). At WR (about +1.1) and TE (about +0.5) it was ahead on average but not reliably. What it is not good at: choosing between players you already own. Among strong RBs it did slightly worse than just trusting the season average, so for that call lean on the "So far" column and your own judgment.

From Week 5 on, a small ▲ or ▼ with a number beside a rank means the player moved that many places since the previous week's run of the same type (Tuesday to Tuesday, Friday to Friday). It appears only when his chance moved 10 or more points, because small rank changes are mostly noise. It describes what changed; in past seasons it was not a buy or sell signal.

The models are frozen for the season. v0.2 is the main version. v0.3 adds last season's points per game and runs alongside it (QB/RB/WR/TE only); in the backtest it helped WR and TE and early-season QB. Both have their own track record.
